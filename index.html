Research

Latent Express Traversal for Manifold Denoising

Faculty advisors: John Wright and Szabolcs Márka

Sep 2026 – present · Ongoing research

I lead development of the theoretical framework for manifold denoising using learned low-dimensional representations and geometric search. My analysis connects reconstruction accuracy to representation, noise, tangent-estimation, and discretization errors. I am investigating whether redundant shortcut connections can be consolidated to reduce computation while preserving the geometric coverage needed for accurate reconstruction, alongside experiments comparing reconstruction quality, memory usage, and inference cost with neural-autoencoder inference.

TpopT: Efficient Trainable Template Optimization on Low-Dimensional Manifolds

J. Yan, S. Wang, X. R. Wei, J. Wang, Z. Márka, S. Márka, and J. Wright

Mar 2023 – Jun 2024 · arXiv preprint, 2023

TpopT replaces exhaustive template-bank search with trainable optimization on a low-dimensional signal manifold. I led the core theoretical analysis, establishing curvature-dependent convergence guarantees for Riemannian gradient descent under Gaussian noise and estimation bounds governed primarily by intrinsic dimension. I independently designed the proof strategy, establishing local linear convergence to a noise-dependent neighborhood and accuracy bounds with only logarithmic dependence on ambient dimension. The analysis quantifies the computational advantage over matched filtering as target accuracy increases. I presented this work at the NSF AI Institute in Dynamic Systems’ Machine Learning for Science & Engineering Workshop in 2023.

Neural Network Theory: Training Dynamics and the Neural Tangent Kernel

T. Wang, X. R. Wei, and J. Wright

Apr 2022 – Apr 2023 · Collaborative theoretical research

I developed components of a time-dependent approximate neural tangent kernel (NTK) framework for finite-width ReLU networks, deriving feature-perturbation bounds and analyzing activation-pattern changes. In related work on noisy manifold observations, I contributed proofs involving local geometry, smooth-function approximation, and small-norm kernel certificates.

I also implemented PyTorch experiments on synthetic circle and sinusoidal manifolds, tracking empirical NTKs during training to examine within- and between-class structure and responses to local input perturbations. The broader convergence analysis remains ongoing.

Adaptive Statistical Learning and Multi-Task Learning

Research with Kaizheng Wang

Aug 2022 – Jun 2024 · Research project

I studied Gaussian multi-task estimation to characterize when pooling related tasks improves on separate estimation, deriving the bias–variance tradeoff and the role of task heterogeneity. In related work, I derived K-fold cross-validation estimators for regularized Gaussian mean estimation and evaluated their risk against maximum-likelihood, Bayesian, and oracle benchmarks using analytical calculations and Monte Carlo experiments.

End-to-End Variational Inference for Portfolio Construction

X. R. Wei, G. Costa, and G. N. Iyengar

Apr 2023 – Jun 2024 · Research project; poster presentation, 2023

I derived task-loss gradients through constrained portfolio optimization using KKT-based implicit differentiation and contributed to the PyTorch implementation connecting Monte Carlo samples from a variational generative model to differentiable optimization. In synthetic-data experiments, I compared end-to-end learning with predict-then-optimize and direct allocation baselines, varying prediction and task-loss weights to study trade-offs between predictive accuracy and portfolio performance. The model architecture was developed collaboratively.

Financial Time-Series Forecasting with Text Sentiment

Columbia University · CSUREMM

May 2021 – Aug 2021 · Paid summer research · Team project

As part of a team in Columbia’s Summer Undergraduate Research Experiences in Mathematical Modeling (CSUREMM), I contributed to a financial time-series forecasting project incorporating text sentiment. Our workflow included sentiment-feature processing and time-series preprocessing with Fourier-based smoothing, normalization, and temporal shifts. We implemented and compared LSTM and CNN-LSTM models in Python/Keras, evaluating predictions using mean absolute error (MAE) on held-out test data.

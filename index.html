CV/Email/Research/Personal

I am a quantitative analyst at Capula Investment Management. I am also a co-founder of Lokin, a platform for trading research and backtesting, and collaborate on machine-learning research at Columbia University. I received my B.S. in Computer Science from Columbia University in 2024, summa cum laude. My research advisors include John Wright, Szabolcs Márka, Kaizheng Wang, and Garud Iyengar.

I study how structure in data and learned representations shapes learning and inference, combining mathematical analysis with empirical investigation to develop better algorithms. My research spans neural-network dynamics, geometric optimization, adaptive statistical learning, and decision-focused optimization, with an emphasis on computational efficiency and reliable performance.

I grew up in Hebei, China, moved to Sydney at 15, and later moved to New York for college and work. I am currently living and working in London. Outside work and research, I am a professional Latin dancer.

Research

Latent Express Traversal for Manifold Denoising

Faculty advisors: John Wright and Szabolcs Márka

Sep 2026 – present · Ongoing research

I evaluate geometric denoising as an alternative to neural-autoencoder inference, comparing reconstruction accuracy, memory usage, and inference cost. I developed a more efficient search graph by consolidating redundant connections and led the supporting theoretical analysis of when geometric search can preserve reconstruction accuracy.

TpopT: Efficient Trainable Template Optimization on Low-Dimensional Manifolds

Faculty advisor: John Wright

Mar 2023 – Jun 2024 · arXiv preprint, 2023

I led the theoretical analysis of trainable optimization on low-dimensional signal manifolds, establishing local convergence and noise-dependent accuracy guarantees for Riemannian gradient descent. The results explain how intrinsic geometry can reduce the computational cost of signal reconstruction relative to exhaustive template search.

Neural Network Theory: Training Dynamics and the Neural Tangent Kernel

Faculty advisor: John Wright

Apr 2022 – Apr 2023 · Collaborative theoretical research

I developed feature-perturbation bounds and analyzed activation changes in a time-dependent neural tangent kernel framework for finite-width ReLU networks. Complementary proofs and PyTorch experiments on synthetic manifolds examine how local geometry and evolving kernel structure influence learning.

Adaptive Statistical Learning and Multi-Task Learning

Faculty advisor: Kaizheng Wang

Aug 2022 – Jun 2024 · Research project

I studied when pooling related tasks improves statistical estimation and how task heterogeneity changes the bias–variance tradeoff. Analytical results and Monte Carlo experiments evaluate multi-task estimation and cross-validation against classical, Bayesian, and oracle benchmarks.

End-to-End Variational Inference for Portfolio Construction

Faculty advisor: Garud Iyengar

Apr 2023 – Jun 2024 · Research project; poster presentation, 2023

I study an end-to-end learning framework that integrates variational inference with differentiable portfolio optimization, allowing downstream decision objectives to guide distribution learning. My contributions connect probabilistic modeling with constrained optimization and examine the relationship between predictive accuracy and decision quality, supported by comparisons with predict-then-optimize and direct-allocation approaches.
